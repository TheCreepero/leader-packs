## joseph_de_goislard_de_monsabert

Joseph de Goislard de Monsabert (1887-1981), French Army; division and corps commander.

- Born 30 September 1887 in Libourne, died 13 June 1981 in Dax. Sources: https://fr.wikipedia.org/wiki/Joseph_de_Goislard_de_Monsabert (Joseph de Goislard de Monsabert); https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Joseph_de_Goislard_de_Monsabert (Joseph de Goislard de Monsabert)
- Colonel in June 1937; in September 1939 commanding the 9th Algerian Tirailleur Regiment (9e RTA) at Miliana. Source: https://www.defense.gouv.fr/chemins-memoire/histoire-memoires/personnalites/personnalites-lettre-e-lettre-m/joseph-goislard-monsabert (Joseph Goislard de Monsabert)
- Served in the Army of Africa under Vichy; general in 1941; Vichy sanctioned him for preparing the Army of Africa for Giraud's arrival in November 1942. Source: https://www.defense.gouv.fr/chemins-memoire/histoire-memoires/personnalites/personnalites-lettre-e-lettre-m/joseph-goislard-monsabert (Joseph Goislard de Monsabert)
- 25 November 1942: took command of the Corps francs d'Afrique, improvised light units of the Tunisian campaign. Source: https://fr.wikipedia.org/wiki/Joseph_de_Goislard_de_Monsabert (Joseph de Goislard de Monsabert)
- March 1943: commander of the 3rd Algerian Infantry Division (3e DIA) as major general; trained it through the summer of 1943. Source: https://www.defense.gouv.fr/chemins-memoire/histoire-memoires/personnalites/personnalites-lettre-e-lettre-m/joseph-goislard-monsabert (Joseph Goislard de Monsabert)
- Italy 1944 with Juin's French Expeditionary Corps: took the Belvedere ridge, Castelforte on 12 May, crossed the Aurunci mountains and reached Siena on 3 July. Source: https://www.defense.gouv.fr/chemins-memoire/histoire-memoires/personnalites/personnalites-lettre-e-lettre-m/joseph-goislard-monsabert (Joseph Goislard de Monsabert)
- 16 August 1944: landed in Provence; his division took part in the capture of Toulon (21 August), then took Marseille (28 August). Source: https://www.defense.gouv.fr/chemins-memoire/histoire-memoires/personnalites/personnalites-lettre-e-lettre-m/joseph-goislard-monsabert (Joseph Goislard de Monsabert)
- 31 August 1944: commander of the French II Corps; through the Vosges and Alsace, the defence of Strasbourg in the winter of 1944-45, across the Rhine to Stuttgart. Sources: https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Joseph_de_Goislard_de_Monsabert (Joseph de Goislard de Monsabert); https://www.defense.gouv.fr/chemins-memoire/histoire-memoires/personnalites/personnalites-lettre-e-lettre-m/joseph-goislard-monsabert (Joseph Goislard de Monsabert)
- July 1945: first commander of the French occupation troops in Germany, general of the army. Sources: https://fr.wikipedia.org/wiki/Joseph_de_Goislard_de_Monsabert (Joseph de Goislard de Monsabert); https://www.defense.gouv.fr/chemins-memoire/histoire-memoires/personnalites/personnalites-lettre-e-lettre-m/joseph-goislard-monsabert (Joseph Goislard de Monsabert)

## marcel_carpentier

Marcel Maurice Carpentier (1895-1977), French Army; staff officer and division commander.

- 1937: head of services at the High Commissioner's office in the Levant; 1939: head of the third (operations) bureau on General Weygand's staff. Source: https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Marcel_Carpentier (Marcel Carpentier)
- 1940-41: chief of staff to de Lattre, commander-in-chief in North Africa under Vichy; joined the Allies in 1942. Source: https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Marcel_Carpentier (Marcel Carpentier)
- 1942-43: commanded the 7th Moroccan Tirailleur Regiment in the Tunisian campaign. Source: https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Marcel_Carpentier (Marcel Carpentier)
- 1943: Juin's chief of staff in the Italian campaign. Source: https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Marcel_Carpentier (Marcel Carpentier)
- September 1944 to 1945: commander of the 2nd Moroccan Infantry Division (2e DIM), which advanced into Germany. Source: https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Marcel_Carpentier (Marcel Carpentier)
- Lived 1895-1977. Source: https://commons.wikimedia.org/wiki/File:General_Marcel_Maurice_Carpentier_in_1944_(cropped).jpg (General Marcel Maurice Carpentier in 1944 (cropped).jpg)

## charles_mast

Emmanuel Charles Mast (1889-1977), French Army; corps chief of staff and division commander.

- Born 7 January 1889 in Paris, died 30 September 1977 in Clamart. Sources: https://fr.wikipedia.org/wiki/Charles_Mast (Charles Mast); https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Charles_Mast (Charles Mast)
- From 1937: military attaché in Tokyo, as a colonel. Source: https://fr.wikipedia.org/wiki/Charles_Mast (Charles Mast)
- 1 June 1940: chief of staff of the 10th Army Corps as temporary brigadier general; captured by the Germans in June 1940 and held at Königstein. Sources: https://fr.wikipedia.org/wiki/Charles_Mast (Charles Mast); https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Charles_Mast (Charles Mast)
- 1941: imprisoned on suspicion of opposing the Vichy regime. Sources: https://fr.wikipedia.org/wiki/Charles_Mast (Charles Mast); https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Charles_Mast (Charles Mast)
- After his release: commander of the Algiers Division, then of the 3rd North African Infantry Division. Source: https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Charles_Mast (Charles Mast)
- 1942: chief of staff of the 19th Army Corps and commander of the Algiers garrison; one of the first Vichy-era commanders to work with the Allies, he helped prepare the Allied landings in North Africa and met General Mark Clark secretly at Cherchell on 23 October 1942. Sources: https://fr.wikipedia.org/wiki/Charles_Mast (Charles Mast); https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Charles_Mast (Charles Mast)
- Mark Clark regarded him as Giraud's spokesman; he mediated between Giraud and de Gaulle on military questions. Sources: https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Charles_Mast (Charles Mast); https://fr.wikipedia.org/wiki/Charles_Mast (Charles Mast)
- 1943: chief of the military missions in Syria and Egypt; 10 May 1943 to 22 February 1947: Resident General in Tunisia. Sources: https://fr.wikipedia.org/wiki/Charles_Mast (Charles Mast); https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Charles_Mast (Charles Mast)

## robert_jaujard

Robert Jaujard (1896-1977), French Navy; cruiser and cruiser division commander.

- Born 6 March 1896 in Saint-Martin-de-Saint-Maixent, died 25 January 1977 in Toulon. Sources: https://fr.wikipedia.org/wiki/Robert_Jaujard (Robert Jaujard); https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Robert_Jaujard (Robert Jaujard)
- Capitaine de frégate from April 1937; from April 1937 commanded the destroyer Vauquelin in the Mediterranean. Source: https://fr.wikipedia.org/wiki/Robert_Jaujard (Robert Jaujard)
- 1940: second-in-command of the cruiser Algérie, noted for his composure and skill in the bombardment of Genoa and Vado. Sources: https://fr.wikipedia.org/wiki/Robert_Jaujard (Robert Jaujard); https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Robert_Jaujard (Robert Jaujard)
- July 1941: capitaine de vaisseau and first deputy chief of staff of the Southern Maritime Forces at Algiers. Sources: https://fr.wikipedia.org/wiki/Robert_Jaujard (Robert Jaujard); https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Robert_Jaujard (Robert Jaujard)
- January 1943: commander of the cruiser Georges Leygues with the Allied French forces; on 13 April 1943 she intercepted the German blockade runner Portland in the South Atlantic. Sources: https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Robert_Jaujard (Robert Jaujard); https://fr.wikipedia.org/wiki/Robert_Jaujard (Robert Jaujard)
- March 1944: rear admiral; May 1944: commander of the IV Cruiser Division. Sources: https://fr.wikipedia.org/wiki/Robert_Jaujard (Robert Jaujard); https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Robert_Jaujard (Robert Jaujard)
- 6 June 1944: his IV Cruiser Division bombarded German positions at Port-en-Bessin in the Normandy landings. Source: https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Robert_Jaujard (Robert Jaujard)
- August 1944: took part in the Provence landings, then commanded the Flank Force of cruisers and submarine chasers supporting the Allied advance along the Italian coast. Sources: https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Robert_Jaujard (Robert Jaujard); https://fr.wikipedia.org/wiki/Robert_Jaujard (Robert Jaujard)
