## antoine_bethouart
Name: Antoine Béthouart / Antoine Bethouart
Lived: 1889-1982 (b. 17 Dec 1889 Dole, d. 17 Oct 1982 Fréjus). Source: https://fr.wikipedia.org/wiki/Antoine_B%C3%A9thouart (Antoine Béthouart); same dates https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Antoine_B%C3%A9thouart (Antoine Béthouart)
1936-1939:
- 1930-1938 deputy military attaché in Yugoslavia, then lieutenant-colonel (date not given) heading the military mission there; then 3rd bureau of the army staff. Source: https://fr.wikipedia.org/wiki/Antoine_B%C3%A9thouart (Antoine Béthouart)
- April 1938: took command of the 5e demi-brigade de chasseurs alpins; at the outbreak of war he was in the Alps, then on the Maginot Line in Moselle. Source: same
- Promoted colonel in 1937 per en.wikipedia (conflicts with fr.wikipedia's lieutenant-colonel until 1938; rank date UNVERIFIED). Source: https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Antoine_B%C3%A9thouart (Antoine Béthouart)
Commands:
- 9 Apr 1940 général de brigade; Feb 1940 formed a high-mountain brigade; Narvik: 13 May Bjerkvik, 28 May retook Narvik; evacuated 7 Jun 1940. Source: https://fr.wikipedia.org/wiki/Antoine_B%C3%A9thouart (Antoine Béthouart)
- 1942 Rabat subdivision then Casablanca division; 8 Nov 1942 arrested, freed after four days; général de division (12 Nov 1942 per rank list). Source: same
- Nov 1943: promoted général de corps d'armée (lieutenant general) and made chief of national defence staff in Algiers. Source: same; en.wikipedia confirms "Lieutenant general: November 1943".
- CORPS COMMAND DATE: Dec 1943 is NOT supported. He took command of the 1er corps d'armée on 1 Sep 1944 (en.wikipedia: "1 September 1944", French I Corps in Armée B); fr.wikipedia says August 1944, day not given, replacing General Martin. Source: https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Antoine_B%C3%A9thouart (Antoine Béthouart); https://fr.wikipedia.org/wiki/Antoine_B%C3%A9thouart (Antoine Béthouart)
- Mid-Nov 1944 attack through the Belfort Gap, Rhine reached 19 Nov, Mulhouse liberated next day; 16 Apr 1945 Rhine crossing; 21 Apr Danube; 6 May 1945 Arlberg pass. Source: fr.wikipedia page above
- Post-war: commander of French forces in Austria, High Commissioner 1946-1950; général d'armée 1948. Source: same
Facts for stats:
- Mountain specialist: professor at Centre d'études de montagne 1925-1928, commanded 24e bataillon de chasseurs alpins, wrote "Le livre de l'Alpin" (1933). Source: https://fr.wikipedia.org/wiki/Antoine_B%C3%A9thouart (Antoine Béthouart)
- Staff and diplomatic skill: École de guerre 1920-1922, 12th corps staff, army staff 3rd bureau, Yugoslav mission, head of military mission to Washington (Jan 1943). Source: same
- Offensive record: Belfort Gap thrust to the Rhine in four days (Nov 1944), Rhine to Arlberg in spring 1945. Source: same
- Wounded three times and cited three times in WWI; Eisenhower praised him in "Croisade en Europe". Source: same
Notes: Rank before 1938 disagrees between wikipedias; the corps command start is Aug/1 Sep 1944.

Source: https://fr.wikipedia.org/wiki/Joseph_de_Goislard_de_Monsabert (Joseph de Goislard de Monsabert); https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Joseph_de_Goislard_de_Monsabert (Joseph de Goislard de Monsabert). The "disagreement" on the death day was not found in these two pages (other sources UNVERIFIED).
1936-1939:
- Colonel in 1937 (en.wikipedia). At the start of WWII commanded the 9e régiment de tirailleurs algériens at Miliana (no date). Interwar garrison postings Tadla, Taza, Blida (undated). Rank/post on 1 Jan 1936 UNVERIFIED. Source: fr.wikipedia and en.wikipedia pages above
Commands:
- Général in Aug 1941 (fr.wikipedia). 25 Nov 1942 took command of the corps francs d'Afrique; then formed and trained 3e DIA, led it through about six months in Tunisia to Tunis. Source: fr.wikipedia
- Nov 1943-Jul 1944 3e DIA in Italy; Aug 1944 Provence landing, liberation of Toulon and Marseille. Source: fr.wikipedia
- 31 Aug 1944 took command of French II Corps in Armée B (en.wikipedia). Winter 1944-45 defence of Strasbourg; crossed the Rhine and took Stuttgart (no dates). Source: both pages
- Jul 1945-Sep 1946 first commandant supérieur of French occupation troops in Germany. Source: fr.wikipedia
Facts for stats:
- Elba and Colmar: NOT mentioned on either fetched page. UNVERIFIED. Vosges appears nowhere either.
- Helped create the corps francs d'Afrique (improvised light units, Tunisia 1942-43). Source: fr.wikipedia page above
- Infantry (tirailleurs algériens) background; no speciality stated. Source: same

## diego_brosset
Name: Diego Brosset / Diego Brosset
Lived: 1898-1944 (b. 3 Oct 1898 Buenos Aires, d. 20 Nov 1944 Champagney, Haute-Saône). Source: https://fr.wikipedia.org/wiki/Diego_Brosset (Diego Brosset). defense.gouv.fr gives birth year 1898 only. Source: https://www.defense.gouv.fr/chemins-memoire/histoire-memoires/personnalites/personnalites-lettre-lettre/diego-brosset (Portrait de Diego Brosset)
1936-1939:
- 1934-1936 commanded the 29e goum at Imiteq near Tiznit; until 1937 native affairs officer in southern Morocco, commanding the Akka sector; 1937-1939 École supérieure de guerre; last stated rank before the war captain (1930). Anticonformist, which cost him a corps staff posting in 1939. Source: https://fr.wikipedia.org/wiki/Diego_Brosset (Diego Brosset)
Commands:
- Jan 1943 2e brigade coloniale (Libya, Tunisia; Takrouna action credited). 1 Aug 1943 1re Division française libre succeeding Koenig, général de brigade. Spring 1944 Italy: Garigliano, Pontecorvo, Hitler Line, Rome. 16 Aug 1944 Provence; Lyon 3 Sep, Autun 8 Sep; général de division. 20 Sep-19 Nov 1944 Vosges. Source: fr.wikipedia page above
- Briefly chief of staff to General Catroux in Eritrea. Source: defense.gouv.fr page above
Cause of death (two sources agree it was an accident, not combat):
- fr.wikipedia: his Jeep skidded on the Rahin bridge and fell into a torrent; aide-de-camp Jean-Pierre Aumont and the driver survived.
- defense.gouv.fr: the car overturned into the river at the Rahin bridge in Champagney during the offensive of 20 Nov 1944; body found 23 Nov. Sources above.
- Caveat: a search summary suggested a French Wikipedia mirror classes him as killed in action, and that a Larminat tribute says he died at the wheel of his jeep. Treat "accident" as correct; date 20 Nov 1944 (fr.wikipedia) versus body found 23 Nov (defense.gouv.fr).
Facts for stats:
- Saharan camel-mounted (méhariste) officer in Mauritania, southern Algeria, southern Morocco, French Sudan; goum commander. Source: fr.wikipedia
- Levant (eastern Syria, 1941), North Africa, Italy. Source: fr.wikipedia
- Hands-on leadership: said he ran his division like a company, climbed onto moving tanks. Source: fr.wikipedia
- Four citations in WWI; Larminat called him "l'homme aux dix citations" (search summary only, UNVERIFIED on a fetched page). Source for four WWI citations: defense.gouv.fr page above
- Vichy sentenced him to death in absentia in April 1941. Source: fr.wikipedia
Notes: Compagnon de la Libération (decree 20 Nov 1944 per search summary; UNVERIFIED on fetched page).

## raoul_magrin_vernerey
Name: Raoul Magrin-Vernerey dit Monclar / Raoul Magrin-Vernerey (Monclar)
Lived: 1892-1964 (b. 7 Feb 1892 Budapest, d. 3 Jun 1964 Paris, Val-de-Grâce). Source: https://fr.wikipedia.org/wiki/Raoul_Magrin-Vernerey (Raoul Magrin-Vernerey). Theatrum Belli confirms death 3 Jun 1964 Val-de-Grâce: https://theatrum-belli.com/in-memoriam-general-raoul-magrin-vernerey-alias-monclar-compagnon-de-la-liberation-decede-le-3-juin-1964/ (In memoriam général Raoul Magrin-Vernerey)
1936-1939:
- Chef de bataillon since 1928, lieutenant-colonel 25 Jun 1938. Rank on 1 Jan 1936 is therefore chef de bataillon (inferred; neither page states the post on that date). Foreign Legion: 2e REI Morocco, then 5e REI Tonkin; Jan 1938 commanded the instruction battalion at Saïda; Jun 1938 returned to Morocco with 4e REI. Commandeur of the Légion d'honneur 29 Jun 1939. Source: fr.wikipedia page and Theatrum Belli page above
Commands:
- 23 Feb 1940 (Theatrum Belli; fr.wikipedia says Feb 1940) took the two Legion battalions forming the 13e DBLE (from 3e REI). Narvik 28 May-2 Jun 1940, "la seule victoire française de 1939-1940". Source: both pages
- 1941 commanded the Free French Brigade of the Orient; Eritrea: forces took Massawa, capturing the Italian admiral and about 14,000 men. Source: Theatrum Belli page
- Oct 1941 delegate and commander of troops of the Alaouite territory. Source: Theatrum Belli page
- Nov 1943 to end of war deputy to the commander-in-chief of Levant troops; May-Jun 1945 helped pacify northern Syria; citation of 12 Apr 1946 names him deputy general for the Levant events of 1945. Source: same; fr.wikipedia agrees on Nov 1943 and May-Jun 1945
- 1946 major general, moved to Algeria; 1950 gave up his general's stars to command the French UN battalion in Korea. No later Levant command. Source: Theatrum Belli page
Facts for stats:
- Wounded seven times; discharged with 90% disability ("blessé sept fois"). CONFIRMED. Source: https://fr.wikipedia.org/wiki/Raoul_Magrin-Vernerey (Raoul Magrin-Vernerey); Theatrum Belli page
- Legendary courage and "coup d'oeil"; refused to fight Frenchmen in Gabon 1940 and Syria 1941; "Bayard du XXe siècle"; one of the most decorated French officers. Source: both pages
- Legion infantry specialist (Legion). Bir Hakeim: not mentioned on either page, UNVERIFIED.
Notes: The Ordre de la Libération page was not fetched. The idea of the Legion paratrooper battalions is presented as legend only.

## philippe_auboyneau
Name: Philippe Auboyneau / Philippe Auboyneau
Lived: 1899-1961 (b. 9 Nov 1899 Constantinople, d. 22 Feb 1961 Paris 16e). CONFIRMED on fetched pages. Source: https://fr.wikipedia.org/wiki/Philippe_Auboyneau (Philippe Auboyneau); https://www.ordredelaliberation.fr/fr/compagnons/philippe-auboyneau (Philippe Auboyneau, Ordre de la Libération); https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Philippe_Auboyneau (Philippe Auboyneau)
1936-1939:
- After the École de guerre navale: sous-chef d'état-major of the Atlantic torpedo-boat flotilla; then capitaine de corvette and deputy chief of staff of the Naval Forces of the Far East at Saigon, where he was when war was declared in 1939. No dates for 1936-1939 on any page. Source: fr.wikipedia page above
- Pre-war: École navale 1917; about ten years in Middle and Far East; commanded hydrographic ship Alidade and gunboat Doudart de Lagrée on the Yangtze (1920s). Source: Ordre de la Libération page
Commands:
- 1939-40 liaison missions with the British Admiralty; Jul 1940 aboard HMS Warspite, architect of the Cunningham-Godfroy "Gentlemen Agreement" at Alexandria; joined the Free French 20 Jul 1940. Source: fr.wikipedia and Ordre pages
- Le Triomphant: commanded after arriving in London in July 1940; later capitaine de vaisseau and commander of Free French naval forces in the Pacific aboard her. NO command dates on any fetched page (UNVERIFIED). The Ordre page calls her a light cruiser ("croiseur léger"), fr.wikipedia and en.wikipedia call her a contre-torpilleur/destroyer; she was a large destroyer (contre-torpilleur) and the cruiser label is a source disagreement. Source: pages above
- Nauru and Ocean Island (Banaba) raid to evacuate the garrisons, with the Australian fleet, after Japan entered the war: placed in 1941-1942 (Ordre page "Pacific, 1941-1942"); exact date UNVERIFIED. He returned to London in April 1942. Source: Ordre page; en.wikipedia
- 1942-1944 commanded Free French naval forces in London and helped merge with the North African navy; Aug 1944 led the 3rd Cruiser Division in the Provence landings; 1945 vice-admiral, commanded naval forces in the Far East; from 1955 commander-in-chief of naval forces in the Mediterranean. Source: Ordre page
Facts for stats:
- Diplomatic and staff skill (Alexandria agreement, liaison with the Admiralty). Source: fr.wikipedia page
- Cruiser Division command in the Provence landings (Aug 1944). Source: Ordre page
- Coastal/river service: Yangtze gunboat. Source: Ordre page
Notes: Vichy stripped him of French nationality in 1940. A figure in the 13 May 1958 events in Algiers (fr.wikipedia). No war-crimes note found.
