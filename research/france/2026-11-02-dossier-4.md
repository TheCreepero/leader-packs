## robert_jaujard
Used: 2026-11-02-france.md
Name: Robert Jaujard (ASCII: Robert Jaujard)
Lived: 1896-1977 (born 6 March 1896, Saint-Martin-de-Saint-Maixent; died 25 January 1977, Toulon). Source: https://fr.wikipedia.org/wiki/Robert_Jaujard (Robert Jaujard); https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Robert_Jaujard (Robert Jaujard)
Ids elsewhere: none
Suggested mode: new. No snapshot id (caller-checked).
Suggested role: navy_leader. He commanded a cruiser, the IV Cruiser Division and the Flank Force in 1943-45.
1936-1939:
- Rank on 1 Jan 1936 and in Sept 1939 are NOT stated by either Wikipedia page. They give capitaine de corvette from September 1931 and capitaine de frégate from April 1937. So on 1 Jan 1936 he was a capitaine de corvette (inference from those dates, not a stated fact) and in Sept 1939 a capitaine de frégate (same inference). Source: https://fr.wikipedia.org/wiki/Robert_Jaujard (Robert Jaujard). Rank on those two dates: UNVERIFIED as stated facts.
- Mid-1930s (undated): chief of interior service and security on the battleship Dunkerque, after supervising work on the new École navale in Brest in 1935. Source: https://fr.wikipedia.org/wiki/Robert_Jaujard (Robert Jaujard)
- From April 1937: commanded the destroyer Vauquelin in the Mediterranean. Source: https://fr.wikipedia.org/wiki/Robert_Jaujard (Robert Jaujard). (A wargaming wiki says he began WWII commanding Vauquelin, weak source: https://wiki.wargaming.net/en/Navy:Robert_Jaujard (Navy:Robert Jaujard). End date of the Vauquelin command: UNVERIFIED.)
Commands:
- 1940: second-in-command (first mate, "second") of the cruiser Algérie; noted for composure and skill in the bombardment of Genoa and Vado (Operation Vado). Source: https://fr.wikipedia.org/wiki/Robert_Jaujard (Robert Jaujard); https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Robert_Jaujard (Robert Jaujard). The wargaming wiki says he commanded Algérie in the June 1940 Genoa raid, which conflicts with the Wikipedia "second-in-command"; Wikipedia is trusted more.
- July 1941 (promoted capitaine de vaisseau): first deputy chief of staff ("sous-chef d'état-major") of the Forces maritimes du Sud at Algiers. Source: https://fr.wikipedia.org/wiki/Robert_Jaujard (Robert Jaujard); https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Robert_Jaujard (Robert Jaujard). End date: UNVERIFIED.
- January 1943: commands the cruiser Georges Leygues with the Free French forces ("Forces navales françaises libres" wording per en.wikipedia: "Free French forces"). Source: https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Robert_Jaujard (Robert Jaujard); https://fr.wikipedia.org/wiki/Robert_Jaujard (Robert Jaujard)
- 13 April 1943: Georges Leygues, under his command, sank the German auxiliary cruiser/blockade runner Portland by gunfire in the South Atlantic (fr.wikipedia). Source: https://fr.wikipedia.org/wiki/Robert_Jaujard (Robert Jaujard). Cross-check: the Leygues ship article describes the 13 April interception of Portland, which was scuttled by her crew, and Atlantic patrols from Dakar in early 1943 (per a search summary; page not fetched): https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/French_cruiser_Georges_Leygues (French cruiser Georges Leygues). Wording differs (sunk by gunfire vs scuttled); trust the ship article for the ship's outcome: UNVERIFIED which is exact.
- January 1944: commands the navy at Algiers ("commandant de la marine à Alger"). Source: https://fr.wikipedia.org/wiki/Robert_Jaujard (Robert Jaujard); https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Robert_Jaujard (Robert Jaujard)
- March 1944: contre-amiral. May 1944: commands the IV Cruiser Division (4th cruiser division). Source: both Wikipedia pages above.
- 6 June 1944 (D-Day): IV Cruiser Division bombarded German positions at Port-en-Bessin. Source: https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Robert_Jaujard (Robert Jaujard) states 6 June; fr.wikipedia mentions Port-en-Bessin without the date.
- August 1944: took part in the Provence landings (Operation Dragoon per en.wikipedia). Then commanded the Flank Force ("Force des Flancs"), cruisers and submarine hunters supporting the Allied advance along the Italian coast (en.wikipedia places it after Dragoon; fr.wikipedia dates it 1944-1945 and does not name Dragoon). Source: both Wikipedia pages above.
- April 1945: Georges Leygues and Montcalm sailed from Oran to Scapa Flow under his command (forum, weak source; UNVERIFIED): https://shipstamps.co.uk/forum/viewtopic.php?p=13447 (George Leygues (French cruiser) 1944)
Facts for stats:
- Cruiser-gunnery action: his Georges Leygues sank the blockade runner Portland on 13 April 1943. Source: https://fr.wikipedia.org/wiki/Robert_Jaujard (Robert Jaujard)
- Shore bombardment: IV Cruiser Division at Port-en-Bessin on D-Day. Source: https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Robert_Jaujard (Robert Jaujard)
- Staff post: first deputy chief of staff, Southern Maritime Forces, Algiers, from July 1941. Source: https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Robert_Jaujard (Robert Jaujard)
- Destroyer command (Vauquelin, Mediterranean) from April 1937 and security/interior service on the battleship Dunkerque. Source: https://fr.wikipedia.org/wiki/Robert_Jaujard (Robert Jaujard)
- Noted for composure and skill as Algérie's second-in-command in the Genoa/Vado bombardment, 1940. Source: https://fr.wikipedia.org/wiki/Robert_Jaujard (Robert Jaujard)
- Postwar: NATO admiral for Western Europe (1948) and Central Europe (1951); Eisenhower held him in high esteem. Source: https://fr.wikipedia.org/wiki/Robert_Jaujard (Robert Jaujard)
- École navale training/supervision work in Brest in 1935. Source: https://fr.wikipedia.org/wiki/Robert_Jaujard (Robert Jaujard)
Appearance: no source states it (neither Wikipedia page describes him). Admiral's uniform in 1944-45 photographs; details UNVERIFIED.
Notes:
- VICHY QUESTION (June 1940 to early 1943). Neither Wikipedia page mentions Dakar, Mers-el-Kébir, Syria, Torch or Vichy, and neither says when or how he joined the Allies; the only Free French reference is his January 1943 Georges Leygues command. Confirmed: the 1940 Algérie service was the Genoa/Vado raid against Italy (not against the Allies); from July 1941 he sat on the staff of the Southern Maritime Forces at Algiers, which was then a Vichy-era (Armistice navy) post, so he held a staff job under Vichy for about 16 months or more (the Vichy label is my inference; the pages do not use it). No source puts him at Dakar, Mers-el-Kébir, Syria or Torch. The Georges Leygues as a ship defended Dakar against the British/Free French attack in September 1940 (search summary of the ship article, page not fetched); no source ties Jaujard to the ship then. Reading: not a Vichy-defined commander (no political role, no fighting the Allies is documented), but a Vichy-navy staff officer 1941-42; his Allied-side career 1943-45 is the defining part. The ecole.nav.traditions.free.fr page (officiers_jaujard_robert.htm) failed (certificate error), the Wayback copy is blocked, netmarine.net not tried. The exact date he joined the Allies: UNVERIFIED. Owner decides.
- Rank on 1 Jan 1936 and Sept 1939 are inferences (see above).
- No war-crimes or atrocity note found on either page.

## charles_mast
Used: 2026-11-02-france.md
Name: Charles Mast (full name Emmanuel Charles Mast; ASCII: Charles Mast)
Lived: 1889-1977 (born 7 January 1889, Paris 2e; died 30 September 1977, Clamart). Source: https://fr.wikipedia.org/wiki/Charles_Mast (Charles Mast); https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Charles_Mast (Charles Mast)
Ids elsewhere: none
Suggested mode: new. No snapshot id (caller-checked).
Suggested role: corps_commander. He was a divisional commander (Algiers Division, 3rd North African Division) and corps chief of staff, never an army commander.
1936-1939:
- 1 Jan 1936: rank and post UNVERIFIED (not stated).
- From 1937: military attaché in Tokyo, called "colonel". Source: https://fr.wikipedia.org/wiki/Charles_Mast (Charles Mast)
- Sept 1939: post and rank UNVERIFIED. en.wikipedia says he was chief of staff of the 10th Army Corps at the start of WWII (https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Charles_Mast (Charles Mast)); fr.wikipedia dates that post 1 June 1940 as temporary brigadier general.
Commands:
- 1 June 1940: chief of staff of the 10th corps (général de brigade à titre temporaire, later confirmed). Source: https://fr.wikipedia.org/wiki/Charles_Mast (Charles Mast)
- June 1940: captured by the Germans, held at Königstein fortress. Source: both pages above.
- 1941: imprisoned on suspicion of opposing the Vichy regime; Japanese attaché Colonel Numata reportedly obtained his release. fr.wikipedia dates for captivity and release are internally inconsistent. Source: https://fr.wikipedia.org/wiki/Charles_Mast (Charles Mast); https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Charles_Mast (Charles Mast)
- After release (dates UNVERIFIED): head of the Division d'Alger, then head of the 3rd North African Infantry Division (3e DIA / 3rd North African). Source: both pages above, no dates.
- 1942: chief of staff of the 19th corps in North Africa; also described as chief of staff of the Algiers corps and commander of the Algiers garrison during the Allied landing preparations, including the secret Cherchell meeting with Gen. Mark Clark on 23 October 1942. Source: https://fr.wikipedia.org/wiki/Charles_Mast (Charles Mast); https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Charles_Mast (Charles Mast) (en does not name Torch).
- 1942: commanded the division de marche of Casablanca. Source: https://fr.wikipedia.org/wiki/Charles_Mast (Charles Mast). Date inside 1942 and outcome: UNVERIFIED.
- 1943: chief of military missions in Syria and Egypt. Source: both pages above.
- 10 May 1943 to 22 February 1947: résident général in Tunisia (fr.wikipedia infobox 10 May 1943; its text says de Gaulle appointed him 10 June 1943; en.wikipedia says 10 May 1943, days after Tunis was captured; replaced by Jean Mons). Source: both pages above. Trust 10 May (two of three statements).
Facts for stats:
- Staff officer: chief of staff of the 10th corps (1940), the 19th corps (1942) and the Algiers corps (1942). Source: https://fr.wikipedia.org/wiki/Charles_Mast (Charles Mast)
- Planning/political: helped prepare the Allied landings in North Africa; one of the first Vichy commanders to work with the Allies; Cherchell meeting 23 October 1942. Source: https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Charles_Mast (Charles Mast)
- Mark Clark saw him as spokesman for Giraud and head of French forces in North Africa; Eisenhower held him in high regard; he went between Giraud and de Gaulle and opposed Darlan and Juin. Source: https://fr.wikipedia.org/wiki/Charles_Mast (Charles Mast)
- Garrison and garrison command: Algiers garrison during the landing preparations. Source: https://fr.wikipedia.org/wiki/Charles_Mast (Charles Mast)
- Divisional commands: Algiers Division, 3rd North African Division (infantry). Source: https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Charles_Mast (Charles Mast)
- Tunisia: "vigoureuse reprise en main du Protectorat"; deposed Moncef Bey, banned the Néo-Destour, exiled Bourguiba to Cairo. Source: https://fr.wikipedia.org/wiki/Charles_Mast (Charles Mast)
- Prisoner of the Germans (Königstein) in 1940. Source: https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Charles_Mast (Charles Mast)
- No offensive or defensive combat reputation found: UNVERIFIED. His record is staff and political-military.
Appearance: no source states it. Photo of him with de Gaulle in Tunis is captioned on fr.wikipedia; no description. UNVERIFIED.
Notes:
- Historian Joseph Barthélemy is cited on fr.wikipedia as saying his Vichy-era appointment reflected pro-Pétain views; he was nonetheless suspected by Vichy and jailed in 1941. Dates of the Algiers Division and 3rd North African Division commands are not in either page; the planner should treat the 1941-42 dates as UNVERIFIED.
- His residency in Tunisia (1943-47) was a political/colonial post, not a field command; his 1949 retirement followed the "affaire des généraux" (Indochina report). The Tunisia repression (Moncef Bey deposed, Bourguiba exiled) is documented on fr.wikipedia but is not a war-crimes finding; no conviction found.
- Ended career général de division with rank of commandant d'armée from 20 February 1947; he sat on the Conseil supérieur de la Guerre and directed the IHEDN. Source: https://fr.wikipedia.org/wiki/Charles_Mast (Charles Mast)
