## charles_delestraint
Name: Charles Delestraint / Charles Delestraint
Lived: 1879-1945 (death: 9 April 1945 per the infobox, 19 April per the body text of the same page; Dachau)
Ids elsewhere: none
Suggested mode: new.
Suggested role: corps_commander. Commanded the tank forces of the 7th Army and the 1st armoured group in 1940.
1936-1939: colonel commanding the 3rd Tank Brigade (Montigny-les-Metz; no dates); brigadier general in 1936; moved to the reserve in March 1939. Source: https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Charles_Delestraint (Charles Delestraint). 1 Jan 1936 exact post UNVERIFIED. 1 Sept 1939: recalled to active duty (same page).
Commands:
- 1940 (no date): combat tanks of the 7th Army. Source: same page
- From 2 June 1940: 1st armoured group; attacked the Abbeville pocket and covered the withdrawal. Corps general May 1940. Source: same page
- Aug 1942 to 9 June 1943: organised and commanded the Armée secrète ("Vidal"), chosen by de Gaulle; worked with Jean Moulin. Source: same page
- 9 June 1943: arrested by the Gestapo; Barbie interrogated him; Natzweiler-Struthof then Dachau. Source: same page
Facts for stats:
- Armoured speciality; tank brigade commander in the 1930s. Source: https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Charles_Delestraint (Charles Delestraint)
- Counter-attack on the Abbeville pocket, June 1940, covered the withdrawal. Source: same page
- Organised the Armée secrète (resistance planning and organisation, not a front command). Source: same page
Appearance: Commons file "File:Photo Charles Delestraint - Archives nationales (France).jpg", CC0, 390 x 444 px, dated April 1943 (identity-card photo, credit Archives nationales, ministère de l'Intérieur; frontal, alone). A larger full-card scan "File:Carte d'identité de Charles Delestraint - Archives nationales (France).jpg" is CC0, 1493 x 1000. Source: https://commons.wikimedia.org/w/api.php (imageinfo). Wearing civilian clothes (identity card): UNVERIFIED. The 1943 photo is of him at about age 64.
Notes: Vichy caveat: none; the Vichy government is not mentioned by the page and he was a resistance leader. Not convicted of anything; he died as a Nacht und Nebel prisoner.

Used: 2026-11-02-france.md

Lived: 1895-1977 (per the Commons file description)
Ids elsewhere: none
Suggested mode: new.
Suggested role: corps_commander. Commanded the 2nd Moroccan Infantry Division in 1944-45.
1936-1939: 1936 UNVERIFIED (the page says only that he stayed a captain for a long time after WWI); 1937 head of services at the High Commissioner's office in the Levant; 1939 head of the third bureau on General Weygand's staff. Source: https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Marcel_Carpentier (Marcel Carpentier)

- 1943: Juin's chief of staff in the Italian campaign. Source: same page
- Sept 1944 to 1945: 2nd Moroccan Infantry Division (2e DIM), which advanced into Germany; page names no battles. Source: same page
- Postwar (outside the period): Indochina commander-in-chief 1949-50, NATO. Source: same page
Facts for stats:
- Staff officer for Weygand (1939), de Lattre (1940-41) and Juin (1943 Italy). Source: same page
- Regimental command in the Tunisian campaign. Source: same page
- Infantry division commander from Sept 1944; named battles UNVERIFIED. Source: same page

(Service cinematographique des armees); alone. Larger alternative "File:General-marcel-carpentier-1944.jpg", 391 x 596 px, same source and date. Headwear UNVERIFIED.

## charles_mast
Used: 2026-11-02-france.md
Name: Charles Mast / Charles Mast
Lived: UNVERIFIED (not on the page seen)
Ids elsewhere: none
Suggested mode: new.
Suggested role: corps_commander. Divisional command in Algeria before Torch; Resident General in Tunisia from May 1943 (an administrative, not a field, post).
1936-1939: military attache in Tokyo in 1937; 1939 post UNVERIFIED. Source: https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Charles_Mast (Charles Mast)
Commands:
- Before June 1940: chief of staff of the 10th Army Corps (page infobox, no dates). Source: same page
- June 1940: captured by the Germans (temporary brigadier general). Source: same page
- After his release in 1941: Algiers Division, then 3rd North African Infantry Division (no dates). Source: same page
- 1942: chief of staff 19th Army Corps in North Africa; prepared the Allied landings, secret meeting at Cherchell, villa Teyssier, 23 Oct 1942 (page dates the meeting to October, not November). Source: same page
- 1943: chief of military missions in Syria and Egypt. Source: same page
- 10 May 1943 to 22 Feb 1947: Resident General in Tunisia. Source: same page
Facts for stats:
- Described as one of the first Vichy commanders to collaborate with the Allies to prepare for the naval landings; General Mark Clark regarded him as the spokesman for Giraud. Source: same page
- Mediated between Giraud and de Gaulle on military questions. Source: same page
- Mostly staff posts; no named battle. Source: same page
Appearance: Commons file "File:Charles Mast, 1943.jpg", public domain, 1574 x 3579 px, dated June 1943, photographer Marjory Collins (Library of Congress, digital ID fsa.8d32413), described as "General and Madame Mast. General Mast is the new resident general of Tunisia." The tall crop is probably Mast alone, but it descends from a photo of the couple: open the file to confirm he is alone. Alternatives in the same category (Mast and wife, 4084 x 4319; Mast arriving at Tunis airport 986 x 1024, 8d32404v) are also public domain. Source: https://commons.wikimedia.org/w/api.php (imageinfo); category https://commons.wikimedia.org/wiki/Category:Charles_Mast
Notes: Vichy caveat (flag plainly): commanded divisions in Vichy North Africa and was imprisoned by Vichy in 1941 on suspicion of opposition (page); defining service is Allied (Torch 1942). The page's field-command record is thin: a weak candidate for stats.

1936-1939: Commons file titles show General Mittelhauser at the Conseil Superieur de la Guerre in 1934, at the south-east manoeuvres with Gamelin in Sept 1936 and inspecting at Montpellier in August 1938 (photo descriptions, not a rank statement). Rank and post on 1 Jan 1936 and Sept 1939 UNVERIFIED. Source: https://commons.wikimedia.org/wiki/Category:Eug%C3%A8ne_Mittelhauser (Category: Eugène Mittelhauser)
Commands:
- June 1940: appointed to command French forces in Syria and Lebanon; the page says he first joined the British, then obeyed the French government and joined the Vichy regime. Source: https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Eug%C3%A8ne_Mittelhauser (Eugène Mittelhauser). Whether he commanded the Army of the Levant from 1939: UNVERIFIED
Facts for stats:
- Staff service: chief of staff of the Czechoslovak army 1921-1925 (French mission). Source: same page
- WWI: commanded the 36th Division in June 1918; wounded twice. Source: same page
Appearance: Commons file "Mittelhauser, Eugène Désiré Antoine" (category Eugène Mittelhauser), public domain, 520 x 693 px, "before 1921", author not identified (Czech source); a portrait-type image, so it predates the period (he was about 48). Exact "File:" title not captured: open the category page. Other PD photos exist (Meurisse 1932, 1934, 1936; L'Éclair 1938) at larger sizes but are action or group shots. Source: https://commons.wikimedia.org/wiki/Category:Eug%C3%A8ne_Mittelhauser
Notes: Vichy caveat: the page says he joined the Vichy regime after obeying the government's orders; the sources do not say he fought the Allies, but this is the clearest Vichy flag of the four and the photo is pre-1921.
