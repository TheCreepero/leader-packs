- antoni_chrusciel: corps_commander. skill 2 (a regiment commander in 1939, then de facto commander of the whole Warsaw Uprising and of its Home Army corps in 1944; facts 2, 3, 4). defense 3, his highest (the 82nd Regiment formed a defensive line on the Widawka and took part in the defence of the Modlin Fortress until its capitulation, fact 2; the uprising's defence of Warsaw, fact 4); attack 2 (ordered the Home Army's mobilisation for 1 Aug 1944 and led the uprising, fact 3); planning 2 (Home Army chief of staff, fact 5); logistics 1 (no fact). urban_assault_specialist (the Warsaw Uprising was a city fight, fact 4).

- leopold_okulicki: corps_commander. skill 2 (a brief command of the 7th Infantry Division and last Home Army commander from October 1944 to January 1945, which he dissolved; facts 3, 4). planning 3, his highest (chief of staff of Anders' Army, 1941-42, fact 3; Rowecki sent him to repair the Lwow area's organisation, fact 2); attack 2 (an SOE officer called him "decisive", fact 5; no battlefield offensive is sourced); defense 1 and logistics 1 (no fact). organizer (chief of staff of Anders' Army, fact 3, and tasked to repair the organisational breakdown of the Lwow area, fact 2).

## stefan_rowecki

Stefan Pawel Rowecki "Grot" (1895-1944), Polish Army; brigade commander in 1939, then commander of the Home Army.

- Commanded the 55th Infantry Regiment in Leszno 1930-1935. Source: https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Stefan_Rowecki (Stefan Rowecki)
- Early 1936 commanded the KOP brigade "Podole"; 1938 deputy commander (infantry) of the 2nd Legions Infantry Division in Kielce; June 1939 formed the Warsaw Armoured-Motorized Brigade. Source: https://dzieje.pl/node/106781 (Stefan Rowecki)
- 10 June to 20 September 1939: commanded the Warsaw Armoured-Motorized Brigade (colonel, staff-college graduate) in Army Lublin; on 13 September its attack toward Annopol succeeded, then it retreated toward Lwow; encircled near Tomaszow Lubelski on 16-17 September and fought its last battle there on 17-20 September; the brigade was dissolved on 20 September. Source: https://pl.wikipedia.org/wiki/Warszawska_Brygada_Pancerno-Motorowa (Warszawska Brygada Pancerno-Motorowa)
- Staff-college graduate (plk dypl.); lieutenant colonel dyplomowany in 1926. Sources: https://pl.wikipedia.org/wiki/Warszawska_Brygada_Pancerno-Motorowa (Warszawska Brygada Pancerno-Motorowa); https://dzieje.pl/node/106781 (Stefan Rowecki)
- October 1939: chief of staff and deputy commander of the SZP; 30 June 1940: commander-in-chief of the ZWZ; 14 February 1942: commander of the Home Army (AK). Source: https://dzieje.pl/node/106781 (Stefan Rowecki)
- 1941: organised the sabotage operation "Wachlarz" in the territory east of the pre-war borders. Source: https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Stefan_Rowecki (Stefan Rowecki)
- Arrested by the Gestapo on 30 June 1943 and murdered in Sachsenhausen in August 1944. Source: https://dzieje.pl/node/106781 (Stefan Rowecki)
- Virtuti Militari Gold Cross (1942); Legion of Honour (1937). Source: https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Stefan_Rowecki (Stefan Rowecki)

## antoni_chrusciel

Antoni Chrusciel "Monter" (1895-1960), Polish Army and Home Army; regiment commander in 1939, commander of the Warsaw Uprising.

- January 1937: deputy commander of the 40th Infantry Regiment; March 1938: commanding officer of the 82nd Siberian Infantry Regiment in Brest-Litovsk. Source: https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Antoni_Chru%C5%9Bciel (Antoni Chruściel)
- 1939: his 82nd Infantry Regiment was mobilised in March and formed a defensive line at the Widawka River; from 2 September it fought in the Piotrkow Operational Group of Lodz Army, then retreated toward the Modlin Fortress and took part in its defence until the capitulation. Source: https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Antoni_Chru%C5%9Bciel (Antoni Chruściel)
- With Bor-Komorowski he ordered the full Home Army mobilisation for 17:00 on 1 August 1944. Source: https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Antoni_Chru%C5%9Bciel (Antoni Chruściel)
- 1 August 1944 onward: commanded the Polish forces in Warsaw and was the de facto commander of all the insurgents in the Warsaw Uprising; on 20 September it was reorganised into the Warsaw Home Army Corps; promoted brigadier general on 14 September 1944. Source: https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Antoni_Chru%C5%9Bciel (Antoni Chruściel)
- June to October 1940: on the ZWZ staff; from October 1940 deputy commander of the Warsaw ZWZ region; later chief of staff of the Home Army. Source: https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Antoni_Chru%C5%9Bciel (Antoni Chruściel)
- Virtuti Militari Gold Cross (28 September 1939) and Silver Cross (1921); Cross of Valour four times. Source: https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Antoni_Chru%C5%9Bciel (Antoni Chruściel)
- After the uprising a prisoner in Stalag XIII-D and Oflag IV-C Colditz, liberated in May 1945. Source: https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Antoni_Chru%C5%9Bciel (Antoni Chruściel)

## boleslaw_duch

Boleslaw Bronislaw Duch (1896-1980), Polish Army; division commander in the Polish II Corps.

- Born 15 November 1896; Cross of Valour eight times and the Virtuti Militari. Sources: https://www.polishexilesofww2.org/boleslaw-duch (Boleslaw Duch); https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Boles%C5%82aw_Bronis%C5%82aw_Duch (Bolesław Bronisław Duch)
- 1935-1938: commanded the 73rd Infantry Regiment; colonel in 1938; after the outbreak of the war infantry commander of the 39th Reserve Infantry Division, which he commanded in practice in September 1939. Sources: https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Boles%C5%82aw_Bronis%C5%82aw_Duch (Bolesław Bronisław Duch); https://www.polishexilesofww2.org/boleslaw-duch (Boleslaw Duch)
- Brigadier general on 5 May 1940; major general in 1945. Source: https://www.polishexilesofww2.org/boleslaw-duch (Boleslaw Duch)
- January 1940: commander of the 1st Grenadier Division in France; 1942 to July 1943: commander of the 1st Rifle Brigade, 1st Polish Armoured Division, in Scotland; from the end of July 1943: commander of the 3rd Carpathian Rifle Division, II Polish Corps, in Italy. Source: https://www.polishexilesofww2.org/boleslaw-duch (Boleslaw Duch)
- His division took the ruins of the Monte Cassino monastery and raised the national banner, took part in the liberation of Ancona, and in April 1945 of Bologna. Source: https://www.polishexilesofww2.org/boleslaw-duch (Boleslaw Duch)

## leopold_okulicki

Leopold Okulicki "Niedzwiadek", "Kobra" (1898-1946), Polish Army and Home Army; division commander and last commander of the Home Army.

- September 1939: held various posts in besieged Warsaw. Source: https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Leopold_Okulicki (Leopold Okulicki)
- January 1940: in the underground in the Lodz area, later in the Bialystok and Lwow areas; Rowecki tasked him with repairing the organisational breakdown in the Lwow area; arrested by the NKVD in January 1941. Source: https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Leopold_Okulicki (Leopold Okulicki)
- 1941-1942: chief of staff of Anders' Army and, for a brief period, commander of the 7th Infantry Division of the Polish forces in the East. Source: https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Leopold_Okulicki (Leopold Okulicki)
- July 1944: commander of the 2nd Echelon of the Home Army (Operation Tempest); chief of staff in the Warsaw Uprising; 3 October 1944: commander of the Home Army; dissolved it on 19 January 1945. Source: https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Leopold_Okulicki (Leopold Okulicki)
- Captain Hudson of the SOE described him as "decisive, sincere and clear-minded". Source: https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Leopold_Okulicki (Leopold Okulicki)
- Virtuti Militari Gold and Silver Cross; Cross of Valour four times. Source: https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Leopold_Okulicki (Leopold Okulicki)
- Arrested by the NKVD in February 1945; sentenced in the Trial of the Sixteen; died in the Butyrka prison on 24 December 1946. Source: https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Leopold_Okulicki (Leopold Okulicki)
